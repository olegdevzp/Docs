## 6. 45-Min Screen — Most Frequent Questions (Soft + Light Tech)

> **What recruiters actually test in 45 minutes:** three things at once — **interest** (will you say yes?), **fit** (does your story match the role?), **leveling** (are you truly senior?).  
> Sources: [PhantomCodeAI recruiter screen guide](https://www.phantomcodeai.com/blogs/what-recruiters-ask-software-engineers), [LogRocket senior frontend screening](https://blog.logrocket.com/the-hidden-skills-gap-in-senior-dev-hiring/), [Frontend Atlas behavioral signals](https://frontendatlas.com/guides/behavioral/big-picture).

### 6.1 How a 45-minute call usually flows

| Time | Block | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **0–5 min** | Warm-up | Greetings, AV check, "how are you", where you're based |
| **5–15 min** | Your story | Tell me about yourself, current role, walk through resume |
| **15–30 min** | Motivation + soft skills | Why this company/role, behavioral STAR questions, work style |
| **30–40 min** | Light technical + logistics | Stack, best project, monorepo/Angular, on-site Stockholm, salary, start date |
| **40–45 min** | Your questions + close | You ask 3–4 questions, next steps |

**Rule:** Keep answers **60–90 seconds** unless they ask "go deeper." Seniors **structure** answers; rambling reads as mid-level.

**Prep minimum:** **7–10 STAR stories** you can reuse across themes (you already have 5 in Section 11).

## 12. Senior-Level & Leadership Questions

### 12.1 "How do you define and maintain frontend standards?"

> **Document lightly, enforce with automation:** ESLint/Prettier, PR templates, library owners, ADRs for big choices, lead by example in my own PRs. Standards should help juniors contribute safely, not block speed.

### 12.2 "How do you balance hands-on coding with mentoring?"

> **~60–70% hands-on** (architecture, shared libs, critical paths) and **~30–40%** unblocking, reviews, design discussions. If I'm only in meetings, quality drifts; if I'm only coding, the team doesn't scale.

### 12.3 "How do you experiment with new tools without destabilizing the team?"

> **Spike → measure → adopt or reject → document.** Pilot on one library, compare CI time and developer feedback, then propose team-wide adoption with a migration plan.

## 13. Frontend Standards, Performance, SEO & Micro-Frontends

### 13.1 Performance approach

> Split **load** (lazy routes, bundle analysis, tree-shaking, vendor chunks) and **runtime** (OnPush, virtual scroll, throttled streams). Measure with **realistic data**, not empty-state Lighthouse.

### 13.2 Quality code definition

> **Correct, readable, testable, maintainable** by someone who didn't write it — modular boundaries, typed contracts, predictable state, clear errors, operational readiness (loading states, feature flags).

### 13.3 Micro-frontend vs monorepo — if they probe

| Approach | When it fits | Your angle |
|---|---|---|
| **Nx monorepo** | One org, coordinated releases, shared libs | Your Shipment Company story |
| **Micro-frontends** | Independent teams/deploys, shell + remotes | Same boundary discipline; eager to learn their exact stack |
| **Hybrid** | Monorepo for shared UI; federated apps for legacy | Common in enterprises — ask what Breakwater uses |
